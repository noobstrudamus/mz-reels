# Do adult clothes need to meet a CPSC rule? 16 CFR part 1610 explained

Many sellers assume CPSC rules are only for children's products, so a line of adult hoodies or dresses has nothing to certify.

16 CFR part 1610, the Standard for the Flammability of Clothing Textiles, says otherwise. Its scope (16 CFR 1610.1(b)): it "provides methods of testing the flammability of clothing and textiles intended to be used for clothing, establishes three classes of flammability, sets forth the requirements which textiles shall meet to be classified, and warns against the use of those textiles which have burning characteristics unsuitable for clothing."

And 16 CFR 1610.1(e): "The requirements of this part 1610 shall apply to textile fabric or related material in a form or state ready for use in an article of wearing apparel, including garments and costumes finished for consumer use." Nothing there limits it to children's wear.

## Exceptions and testing exemptions

- 1610.1(c) excludes hats, gloves and footwear under specific conditions (for example, gloves no longer than 14 inches), and interlining fabrics. Read the list for the exact wording.
- 1610.1(d) exempts some fabrics from testing to support guaranties, including plain surface fabrics of 2.6 ounces per square yard or more, and fabrics made entirely of acrylic, modacrylic, nylon, olefin, polyester or wool. A raised-fiber cotton fleece is not on that list. This is only an exemption from testing to support guaranties; not being exempt does not mean a fabric fails.

## What that means for the paperwork

- 16 CFR 1110.5: products subject to a rule enforced by the Commission that are imported or distributed in commerce "must be accompanied by a finished product certificate."
- For adult clothing, that is a General Certificate of Conformity (GCC), defined in 16 CFR 1110.3.
- 16 CFR 1110.11(a)(2): the certificate "must identify separately all applicable rules." An adult-apparel GCC that skips part 1610 has a gap.
