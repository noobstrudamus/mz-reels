# Does the CPSC dresser tip-over rule apply to adult dressers?

A common assumption: the tip-over rule is about kids' furniture, so a dresser marketed for an adult bedroom is outside it.

The rule itself says otherwise. 16 CFR 1261.1(b) defines a clothing storage unit as "any free-standing furniture item manufactured in the United States or imported for use in the United States that is intended for the storage of clothing, typical of bedroom furniture." The same paragraph says the units subject to the rule are those manufactured after September 1, 2023. The definition does not depend on who the dresser is marketed to.

What the rule is for is a different question. 16 CFR 1261.1 says the requirements "are intended to protect children up to 72 months of age from tip-over-related death or injury." Young children live in adult bedrooms too.

## What the rule requires

16 CFR 1261.2: "Each clothing storage unit that is subject to ASTM F2057-23, Standard Safety Specification for Clothing Storage Units, approved on February 1, 2023, shall comply with ASTM F2057-23."

## What that means for the paperwork

- 16 CFR 1110.5: products subject to a rule enforced by the Commission that are imported or distributed in commerce "must be accompanied by a finished product certificate."
- For a dresser sold for general use, that certificate is a General Certificate of Conformity (GCC), not a Children's Product Certificate (16 CFR 1110.3).
- 16 CFR 1110.11(a)(2): the certificate "must identify separately all applicable rules." A dresser GCC that never lists part 1261 has a gap.
- 16 CFR 1110.11(a)(6): it must also give the most recent date and place of testing, including the testing party's contact details.

## Check yours

1. Is it a free-standing clothing storage unit made after September 1, 2023, and within the scope of ASTM F2057-23?
2. Does the GCC list 16 CFR part 1261?
3. Does it show the test date, the test place and who tested it?
